# The History of METRORail
## and the many mistakes that were made.
### by Felipe Woods

The METRORail is Houston's light rail system, one of only two such systems in Texas (the other is in Dallas, which is a 
story for another day). I've used the METRORail at every chance my parents could since I was a child. I remember the 
days of when the Red Line was only a segment of what it currently is, as well as the history of the expansion of the 
system itself, some of which showed some success and major failures. Given my experience (and disappointment in 
hindsight), here is my definitive history of Houston's METRORail system. 

## Predecessors
The history of rail transit in Houston actually dates way back to 1868 with the first horse-drawn trolley line in 
Houston. It was pulled by mule and used what is essentially modified horsecarts on rails. But other than that everything
seems good, right? The city can boast of having the first horsecars produced in the city, increasing pride in the city. 
Only one problem with that though, they built it on the wrong street. At the time, the main commercial thoroughfares in 
Houston were Travis, Main, and Congress streets, due to its proximity to Allen's Landing, a major port 
located in the confluence of the Buffalo Bayou and White Oak Bayou where the University of Houston-Downtown sits today.
